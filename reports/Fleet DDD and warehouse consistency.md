# Fleet design holds while its seams drift

**The ecosystem is consistent in design, inconsistent in upkeep, and on `develop` it appears broken at one seam.** Domain-driven design is genuinely applied: boundaries rest on a declared language, aggregates enforce their rules, the retail network's event contracts agree across 90 types, and the anti-corruption layer is real. The WMS, WES and network concepts are recognisable and mostly sit in the right place. Three things stop me calling it consistent. First, a breaking Vendor API change on 2026-11-01 left `network-fulfillment` decoding the old response shape, so by static reading every purchase-order and inbound poll returns nothing, silently. Second, some domain rules do not do what their names say: allocation has no rule, task interleaving cannot mix task types, and confirmed network units are not held against stock. Third, the site has fallen behind the fleet in ways that contradict itself, because almost every gate compares one copy with another copy and none compares a copy with the code. For a study project the modelling quality is high and most gaps are legitimate. Fix the seam, settle two questions with one real run, and publish a scope list, and the verdict moves to consistent.

>
> **WES.** WES has no book basis at all. ## Scorecard: tactical work is strongest, status and semantics weakest

Ratings are my judgement on a four-step scale of Strong, Good, Mixed and Weak. Missing commercial features do not lower a rating. Contradictions, wrong semantics and overclaims do.

| Area | Rating | One-line reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strategic DDD | **Good** | Real linguistic boundaries and an honest map, but three of five WMS/WES Core verdicts fail the page's own test, and pattern labels are often used against Evans's definitions. |
| Tactical DDD | **Strong** | Encapsulated aggregates, tested layering and an outbox that holds in every sampled repo. Weak spots are `WorkUnit` setters, three event-raising styles, and a "saga" with no compensation after allocation. |
| WMS | **Mixed** | Broad, with sound replenishment, labor and ABC metrics, but allocation has no rule, cycle counts lose SKU grain, and "the core is complete" overclaims. |
| WES | **Mixed** | Waveless pull release is a sound implementation of the pattern. Flow balancing only advises, the "embedded WES" verdict is generous, and the category itself is contested. |
| Network | **Good, with one critical break** | The strongest conceptual model and fully consistent event contracts, but the live vendor client no longer matches the Vendor API, and two stock and capacity leaks undercut the promise claims. |

## What conforms: most of the design survives close reading

**Tactical discipline is real and tested.** A grep of `internal/domain` across 22 repos found 0 imports of SQL, HTTP, Kafka, JSON encoding or logging, and all 22 have an `internal/architecture` directory (`inventory-storage:internal/architecture/architecture_test.go:16`). At least 35 domain types with three or more fields export none, and a scan found no aggregate-to-aggregate field references in the sample. Canvases name real code: 207 of 207 cited errors and 75 of 75 cited tests exist across 10 canvases. Naming that existence proves little by itself, so 8 aggregates were read against their rules, and most held. Across 186 event types none is imperative, and only about 4% are CRUD-like. Stable outbox ids, dedupe in the effect's transaction and full-type dispatch (0 suffix or prefix matches across 21 repos) make this the most consistently implemented part of the fleet. Scripted spec-to-encoder field checks came back at 124 of 124, 35 of 35, 52 of 52 and 87 of 91, with the other 4 confirmed by hand.

**Strategic artifacts are mostly faithful.** All 36 landing-page tiers match their classification pages. The "Same word, different model" section and the network's translation table are the scoping Evans asks for ("Model expressions, like any other phrase, only have meaning in context", [DDD Reference p.4](https://www.domainlanguage.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/DDD_Reference_2015-03.pdf)). All 19 fleet Bounded Context Canvases carry the [ddd-crew](https://github.com/ddd-crew/bounded-context-canvas) sections, and the Starter Process page says plainly that it indexes the site. The hard boundary with the retail network through a single seam is textbook, and sampled direction reciprocity was good.

**Warehouse semantics are right more often than not.** Min/max replenishment with an inventory-position view is correct (`pick-face-replenishment:internal/domain/detection/detector.go`). ABC by pick-lines matches Bartholdi's "the number of pick-lines is an indication of the labor required" (§3.3, p.26, [Bartholdi & Hackman](https://www2.isye.gatech.edu/~jjb/wh/book/editions/wh-sci-0.98.pdf)). Waveless release is a documented decision with its cost admitted (`wes-work-planning` ADR-0002), and capacity as the minimum across constraints is sound bottleneck logic. Frozen labor standards, GS1/SSCC identity and hazmat segregation also hold. Equipment absence is stated and enforced by an empty port. Every quote on `docs/wms-wes/` that could be checked was accurate.

**The network's structure is the best-grounded part.** Inventory and parcel node types, the promise, assignment and order separation, event-fed promise inputs, stop-based wave capacity and measured-not-declared scorecards all hold. Every consumer-named event type exists at its producer with 0 mismatches, and no integration event is orphaned. `inventory-storage` is the sole writer of physical stock, and `offer-availability` holds a vendor claim, a different quantity with a different owner. The in-organisation REST clients checked all agree with their specs.

## (b) Semantics that are wrong: names promising what the model does not do

**Warehouse semantics.** Allocation has no rule: `inventory-storage:reserve_stock.go:234-270` walks units as returned, and `stock_repo.go:73` has no `ORDER BY`, so there is no forward-first, FIFO or FEFO choice and selection depends on row order. The book treats a pick from reserve as the more expensive case (§8.4, p.111), yet a pick can draw from reserve while the pick face has stock.

Task interleaving does not interleave. Bartholdi means "trips that are pairs of stows and retrievals" (§6.2.1, p.60), and the site's own definition is "combine tasks of different types into one trip" (`heat-maps.md:356`). But `fulfillment-execution:claim_next.go:54` scans a single task type, `STOW` is not a task type at all, and the context's pool sees only `RELOCATE` and `REPLENISH` (`context-map.md:401`). The recommender is well built and honest about being advisory, and what it does is same-type proximity.

Cycle counts take one quantity per bin (`inventory-storage:run_cycle_count.go:40,46`), so under chaotic storage a swap of 5 of SKU A for 5 of SKU B reconciles clean. Treat this as conditional: nobody confirmed that mixed bins are reachable, though `glossary.md` says any SKU may occupy any free bin.

**Network semantics.** `RETURNS_CENTER` is unreachable: no lane pair has it as origin or destination (`model.yaml:86,103-115`; `node-model.md:39`), and `ReturnToSender` is a state jump with no movement (`model.yaml:1461`). Capacity is folded into the stock figure without time-phasing, against ATP as the uncommitted, time-phased portion of supply (**[from memory]** APICS), and capacity is then counted again in `PromiseBook`.

**DDD semantics.** The transfer "saga" is an orchestrated process manager with deferred compensation. A rejection path exists, and the e2e feature has a "rejected and compensated, releasing no work" scenario. Compensation after a successful allocation does not exist, and neither does a usable `Cancel`: it is illegal after `ALLOCATING` and has no route or use case (`network-inventory-planning:internal/domain/transfer/saga.go:260-265`; ADR 0015). That falls short of the saga as **[from memory]** Richardson defines it. The project discloses this, so the fault is the label. The e2e header also discloses that `SiteCapabilityChanged` and `SiteSkuDemandChanged` are injected and site custody is seeded, because no producer or write path exists, so the saga is less built than "built" suggests.

Several context-map patterns contradict Evans's definitions ([DDD Reference pp.28-37](https://www.domainlanguage.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/DDD_Reference_2015-03.pdf)). K33 and K35 put `network-inventory-planning` upstream of a command whose shape the receiver defines, inverting the page's own rule (`context-map.md:128-131,389,391`). Conformist plus ACL appears as one pattern (K4, K17, R10, R12, and `network-fulfillment`), though Conformist eliminates translation and ACL performs it. "Customer/Supplier with no influence" (K15) is simply Conformist.

`order_ref` means an order on `TaskCompleted` and a work unit on `TaskCPTMissed` and `PackageManifested`, on one topic, undisambiguated (`fulfillment-execution:apis/asyncapi.yaml:250-254,425-427,740-742`). `OrderRef` is declared "an opaque correlation key", yet K52 and K58 parse it.

The Core criterion is applied unevenly. The page says size and difficulty do not decide (`subdomain-classification.md:11-12`), then argues `inventory-storage` from "30 tested aggregate invariants" and necessity (`:40`), the trap Evans names as "all complicated and all absolutely necessary to success" ([p.40](https://www.domainlanguage.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/DDD_Reference_2015-03.pdf)). `network-inventory-planning` is Core with "no forecasting or optimisation", while slotting is demoted for being deterministic and replaceable behind a port (`:42,:50`). `warehouse-planning`'s outputs move no operating decision (`context-map.md:374,405`). The fleet vision's winning loop also omits two Core contexts and names a Generic/Supporting one (`domain-vision.md:28-35`). Only `wes-work-planning` and the dispatch slice of `fulfillment-execution` clearly pass. The network page has the decisive test (`network/docs/strategic-design/subdomain-classification.md:61-63`).

## (c) Stale or self-contradicting docs: two dated events and one mechanism

**The heat-map staleness and the mid-October strategic staleness share a cause, and the notes support it.** Both are the same two contexts missing from hand-maintained pages, and `heat-maps.md:355` traces to `scripts/capability-map/capabilities.py:631`, which the `--check` gate cannot catch because it compares pages with the model. A git check of mine gives the timing: `capabilities.py`, `docs/wms-wes/qualification.md` and `docs/strategic-design/ubiquitous-language.md` last changed on 2026-10-20 and `docs/glossary.md` on 2026-10-17, while the `returns-processing` and `exception-management` packs arrived on 2026-10-21.

I infer that this rollout, after the docs' last edits, explains the stale routes in `capabilities.py` and the tier pages. `model.yaml`, last changed 2026-10-26, went the same way.

**The mechanism is that each gate compares copy to copy.** `generate.py --check` compares pages with the model. `check_model.py` reports 0 findings while its routes are stale. The network map's `?` passes every gate. The `network-fulfillment` contract test compares a client with its own pinned spec. Generating a page removes hand-rolled contradictions but cannot catch drift from code.

## (e) Unstated scope gaps: fine to omit, but unlabelled they read as oversights

None of these is a defect in a study project. The defect is that a reader of the site cannot tell a decision from a miss, and several sit under pages that claim completeness.

**Warehouse floor.** Directed put-away and quality hold or quarantine are out of scope only in `inbound-receiving` ADR 0001. FEFO/FIFO rotation, batch, cluster and zone picking, pick-path sequencing, cross-docking and cartonization are stated nowhere. Goods-to-person is covered only by the general "no automation" statement, and count scheduling only by the narrow wording of `capabilities.py:275`. `STOW` and count are not task types, so inbound and count labor cannot be released, dispatched, scored or interleaved, which limits the labor and WES claims more than the site admits (`fulfillment-execution:internal/domain/task/task.go:18-23`). Task generation also differs from the site's own stack diagram (`industry-reference.md:56`): the WMS tier supplies no tasks. Yard, VAS/kitting, outbound transportation and WCS are already declared, and they are the right model for the rest.

**Network.** Proof of delivery is absent, with an outcome and timestamp only (`last-mile-delivery:apis/asyncapi.yaml:408-412`). Also unstated: yard management; store and ship-from-store nodes; future supply in the available figure; money in the sourcing cost (ordinal only, `order-sourcing:internal/domain/sourcing/sourcing.go:21-24`); and a driver for load building (HTTP-only `OpenLoad` and `LoadPackage`). No reverse logistics exists. Shipment confirmation is hard-coded to one package (`network-fulfillment:confirm_network_order_shipment.go:16`).

**The joint.** The fleet can only be one node for stock: `recompute_capability_offers.go:47-49` holds one site and `GET /inventory/{sku}/usable` takes no site, though `network-inventory-planning` exists to move stock between sites. The fleet canvas discloses this and the network's integration pages do not. Planning capacity, operational capacity and declared capacity are not connected at the top, and the vendor scorecard has no reader in the fleet.

**Qualification.** Four of nineteen contexts have no WMS/WES verdict. The books read suggest easy closures: WMS extended for `pick-face-replenishment`, `returns-processing` and `task-interleaving`. `exception-management` is the owner's decision.

## Where the notes conflict: five disagreements, stated rather than averaged

**Shared kernel.** The strategic notes verify that no `go.mod` requires a sibling and suggest the map's "no Shared Kernel" should read "no shared code". The tactical notes find `gs1` copied byte-identically into 4 repos under a "DO NOT EDIT" header, and `cloudevents.go` in 22 repos. Both are right at different levels, so even "no shared code" overstates. The accurate wording is "no shared module; an explicit copied kernel for `gs1` and CloudEvents, enforced by the template's drift sensors (not read), and undeclared semantic copies such as `pathcatalog`, CPT and `packlevel`". The map needs that sentence.

**Transfer saga.** The network notes rate it "matches the textbook shape" and say usable stock at the origin drops on reservation. The tactical notes rate it an error for lacking compensation and suspect the reservation never connects to the pick. The forward message flow is sound; compensation after allocation is absent; and if the reservation only lapses, origin usable stock would rise again after it expires, which contradicts the network notes' account. That last point stays open until the run in priority 2.

**Reservation expiry severity.** The tactical notes say error and the WMS/WES notes say debatable. I keep it as a likely defect, unrun: the transfer path visibly has nothing acting on expiry, while the order path's recovery was not traced.

**Vendor node path.** Coordinator verification cited `nodes.go:23` for the `nodes` field. I checked: that line is the local `NETWORK_NODES_FILE` config shape, and the API response struct is `vendorNodeListDTO` at `wire.go:74-75`, decoded at `client_operations.go:43`, as the notes said. The third broken path stands, on the corrected citation. `vendor-connectivity`'s node handler does emit `Items` (`handlers.go:163`). The consequence for node adoption was not traced.

**Severity of the `returns-processing` label.** The fleet says `wes` is "every other context", but `returns-processing` publishes `com.warehouse.wms.*`. The strategic notes call this cosmetic and the WMS/WES notes a page-consistency error. It is a one-line correction in `CLAUDE.md:297` and `qualification.md:23-26`; the label itself stands.

## Conclusion

The sharpest pattern across all four reviews is that consistency tracks enforcement. Where a test holds the rule (layering, type dispatch, event catalogues, encoder fields), the fleet is close to flawless across the 22 repos scanned and three template generations. Where a person holds it (status prose, route citations, pattern labels, counts), it drifted within days of each fleet-wide change. The Vendor API break is the same failure at runtime, and it hit the one client that crosses an organisational boundary while the in-organisation clients checked stayed correct. The lesson worth keeping from this study is that a Published Language with a conformist downstream needs a test that reads the upstream's live contract.

The domain questions cut differently, and they are the ones to decide on purpose. WMS, WES and network concepts are sound individually, and the weak points are the hand-offs: allocation does not use what slotting and replenishment produce, confirmed promises are not held until the warehouse reserves, and transfer reservations may not meet their picks. Those three questions, more than any missing feature, decide whether the loop the vision describes actually closes. The WES question deserves less anxiety. With no book basis and the trade press itself arguing over the category, the defensible claim is narrower than the site's: this fleet implements the WES release pattern on manual work, which is a good thing for a study to show.
