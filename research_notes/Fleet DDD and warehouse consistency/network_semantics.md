## Baseline: how a retail fulfillment network works and how such systems are divided

### Takeaway
The literature and vendor documentation describe a chain of OMS, DOM/sourcing, ATP/CTP promising, TMS, carrier/visibility, YMS and vendor/EDI functions. The retail-network's eleven contexts map onto most of these. The deliberate omissions are YMS, carrier procurement, money, demand planning, reverse logistics and store nodes. Only the last two carry internal contradictions.

### Cited Findings
- `[memory]` Chopra and Meindl treat distribution network design through forms such as manufacturer storage with direct shipping (drop-ship), in-transit merge, distributor storage with carrier or last-mile delivery, and customer pickup, and they classify facility roles by function. Cross-docking is presented as moving product from inbound to outbound with minimal storage.
- Cross-docking means inbound to outbound with little or no storage; some variants allow brief interim storage ([Quivo](https://quivo.co/en/cross-docking-meaning-definition/), [Simfoni glossary](https://simfoni.com/glossary/cross-docking/?llm_view=1)). These are vendor glossaries, consistent with each other.
- ATP answers order inquiries from inventory and planned supply and generates quantities and due dates ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Available-to-promise), [Oracle Supply Chain ATP](https://docs.oracle.com/cd/A60725_05/html/comnls/us/mrp/atp01.htm)). CTP additionally considers capacity and the possibility of producing more ([UpCounsel, weak source](https://www.upcounsel.com/capable-to-promise-definition), [Microsoft order promising](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/previous-versions/dynamicsnav-2016/hh178517(v=nav.90))). `[memory]` APICS defines ATP as the uncommitted portion of inventory and planned production, maintained in a time-phased way; that time-phased property matters for finding N2.
- DOM evaluates nodes live on cost, proximity, inventory and commitments, and splits at line level ([Acquaint, vendor blog](https://acquaintsoft.com/blog/build-distributed-order-management-system)). Sourcing rules include fulfillment constraints, optimizations, max splits and node prioritization, applied each time sourcing runs ([Radial docs](https://docs.radial.com/romhelp/Content/Topics/admin/about-sourcing-rules.htm)). Split logic weighs inventory and cost across scenarios, can cap cost or shipment count, and aims at the fewest shipments ([Fluent Commerce](https://docs.fluentcommerce.com/by-type/split-orders-across-multiple-fulfilment-locations)).
- TMS covers load planning, tendering (EDI 204 or API, with accept, reject and re-tender), freight audit and visibility ([Umbrex](https://umbrex.com/resources/transportation-management-system-playbook/core-tms-capabilities-and-modules/), [Logistics Viewpoints](https://logisticsviewpoints.com/transportation-management-systems-tms-buyers-guide/)).
- Direct-fulfillment vendor EDI runs 850 PO, 855 acknowledgement, 856 ship notice, 810 invoice ([Stacksync, Amazon Direct Fulfillment 850](https://www.stacksync.com/edi/amazon/amazon-dropship-direct-fulfillment/x12-850-purchase-order)). A 3PL relationship uses 940 shipping order, 945 shipping advice, 943 stock transfer shipment advice and 944 receipt advice; 846 reports inventory ([Zenbridge](https://zenbridge.io/insights/3pl-edi), [Racklify: 856 vs 940/945](https://racklify.com/encyclopedia/edi-856-vs-edi-940945-when-to-use-each/)). Sources disagree on the 945's exact recipient.
- An SSCC is an 18-digit number with a check digit computed as for GTINs, carried in a GS1-128 barcode under AI 00. Despatch advice identifies each logistic unit by SSCC, and the label must match the 856 ([GS1 UK](https://www.gs1uk.org/support-training/help-to-use-and-implement-gs1-standards/logistics-labels-and-ssccs), [SPS Commerce](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/labeling-for-new-suppliers-gs1-128-sscc-and-where-labels-have-to-go)).

## Node topology: are node types and roles in line with network-design literature?

### Takeaway
The taxonomy is coherent and strongly justified by the benchmark, but it has one structural defect (no return path anywhere in the lane rules) and minor contradictions. The absence of store and supplier nodes is an unstated scope gap, not an error.

### Cited Findings
- **OK.** Inventory nodes versus parcel nodes is a clean functional split, and the type-pair lane rule enforces the flow FC to sort to station, plus direct injection and air (`model.yaml:103-115`, `network/docs/network-model/node-model.md:25-42`). Regions and service areas (`node-model.md:156-162`) match the regionalised design the benchmark cites (`retail-network-evolution-2026-11.md:14`).
- **OK, code enforces it.** `ErrLaneTypePairNotAllowed` and the capability derivation are in the model; `node-topology:internal/domain/node/types.go:78-86` holds the type rules.
- **error (N10): `RETURNS_CENTER` is unreachable.** `node-model.md:39` says it is on a customer path as "return destination only" and `model.yaml:86` says "The return location a customer return is routed to", yet no lane pair in `model.yaml:103-115` has it as origin or destination. Nothing leaves a delivery station either: every station pair ends at `DELIVERY_STATION`. `ReturnToSender` is a state jump `AT_STATION to RETURNED` with no reverse movement (`model.yaml:1461`). Not registered; the gap register covers only package-level events (G6, G16). `retail-network-evolution-2026-11.md:99` mentions in one line that no context owns the customer return decision, but not the missing lanes.
- **debatable: `INBOUND_CROSS_DOCK` "holds stock".** `node-model.md:25-28` classifies it as an inventory node that "hold[s] stock", which conflicts with the textbook meaning of cross-docking (see Baseline). The benchmark's IXD does buffer, so this is defensible for a study based on that operator, but the page should say the term is used in the operator's sense. Also, no context moves stock IXD to FC: the lane `model.yaml:115` is unused by any flow, and G43 covers seller freight only.
- **cosmetic, doc contradicts model (related to G64).** `node-model.md:37` gives `FULFILLMENT_CENTER` capabilities `OUTBOUND`, while `model.yaml` (`node_types`) and `ubiquitous-language.md:123` give `OUTBOUND, INBOUND`. Code has outbound only (`node-topology:internal/domain/node/types.go:80`), so **G64 is still open**.
- **error, doc overstates the fleet: registration by `siteRole` is not implemented.** `node-model.md:166-169` says the role table "is the registration rule the fleet's network-fulfillment applies". A grep of `network-fulfillment` Go code for `siteRole` returns nothing; nodes come from a hand-written `NETWORK_NODES_FILE` (`network-fulfillment:internal/adapters/outbound/retailnetwork/nodes.go:11-23`). `model.yaml:118` (`fleet_site_role_mapping`) is a design intention, not as-built.
- **scope gap, unstated: no `STORE` or `SUPPLIER` node.** A grep across the model and strategic pages finds no ship-from-store, click-and-collect or BOPIS. `[memory]` Omnichannel texts treat the store as a node and customer pickup as a network form. As a pure-play e-commerce study this is fine, but the omission is not declared on `domain-vision.md` ("What the network is not", lines 74-90). Supplier: vendors own their stock, and upstream replenishment sits inside the vendor. This is declared there as "Not an inventory planner for vendors".
- **debatable: direction of cutoff.** The vendor declares cutoffs and the network then creates one pickup per cutoff (`model.yaml:1215`, PickupFromCutoffs), with truck capacity derived from the vendor's order capacity (`PickupCapacity` policy). `[memory]` Conventionally the carrier or transport schedule sets the warehouse's cutoff, the critical pull time. The model's direction is internally consistent and is justified by "the fleet's CPT is the network's pickup cutoff" (`wms-wes-integration.md:82-87`), but it means a truck never constrains a vendor and a vendor declaration can schedule a network vehicle.

### Inferences
- The node model is the strongest part of the design. The reverse-flow hole is the main real defect.

### Gaps
- Air hub and sort-centre operations are modelled only as `sortDwell` and loads. No context owns a sort plan, which is reasonable for a study but unstated.

## Order sourcing / DOM: is the decision modelled as the literature describes, and is the split from OMS defensible?

### Takeaway
Separating promise, sourcing and order is defensible and well argued: late binding under a promise constraint, re-optimisation until release, and fill-or-kill. The cost function is ordinal (no money, disclosed only in the evolution note), and the split decision sits in `delivery-promise` and is frozen before sourcing, which is an unusual placement that limits what sourcing can do. There is one real accounting defect: when sourcing moves a group off the witness node, `PromiseBook` never follows.

### Cited Findings
- **OK.** `customer-ordering` (OMS) versus `order-sourcing` (DOM) versus `delivery-promise` is argued on `network-model/order-assignment-model.md:20-29` and `adr/0002-promise-and-assignment-are-separate.md`. A promise needs *a* path fast, assignment needs the *best* path late. This fits the DOM literature's "applied each time sourcing runs" ([Radial](https://docs.radial.com/romhelp/Content/Topics/admin/about-sourcing-rules.htm)).
- **OK, code matches.** `order-sourcing:internal/domain/sourcing/sourcing.go` searches candidates, excludes refusers and sets release points. `HandleNodeRefusal` reassigns (`model.yaml` policy ReassignOnRefusal). Constants live at `sourcing.go:21-23`.
- **scope gap, not in the register: cost is not money.** `model.yaml:856` gives `cost = legs + crossRegionPenalty + scorecardPenalty + bookPressurePenalty`. As built: `LegCost=100`, `HourCost=10`, `CrossRegionPenalty=1000`, `BookPressurePenalty=500` (`sourcing.go:21-24`, `sourcing.go:189`; ADR A12 at `order-sourcing:docs/adr/0002-aggregates-invariants-and-assumptions.md:123`). The literature's cost-based node selection ([Fluent](https://docs.fluentcommerce.com/by-type/split-orders-across-multiple-fulfilment-locations)) has no analogue. The gap is acknowledged only in `retail-network-evolution-2026-11.md:23` (F7). Acceptable for a study, but a Core context whose differentiator is cost has no cost unit, and the G-register omits it.
- **debatable (N6): split is decided by `delivery-promise` and never revisited.** `ShipmentGrouping` lives in the promise (`promise-model.md:153-158`; `delivery-promise:internal/domain/search/search.go:137-156`). `model.yaml` order-sourcing decision: "A group goes to one node in full". Sourcing cannot merge two groups that have become co-locatable or re-split a group when no single node remains. The group then goes UNSOURCEABLE and is re-promised. The DOM literature treats line-level splitting as a sourcing decision ([Acquaint](https://acquaintsoft.com/blog/build-distributed-order-management-system)). Defensible since a promise per group must exist before the order, and the vendor's fill-or-kill requires stable groups, but the consequence is unstated. Splitting has no cost term and no cap other than "minimises the number of groups".
- **error (N5): `PromiseBook` tracks the witness node, not the assigned node.** `OnFulfillmentLocked` only records the assignment and judges (`delivery-promise:internal/application/usecases/projections_fulfillment.go:15-27`). `ReleaseConfirmed` is called only from `lifecycle.go:119` (cancel) and `risk.go:232` (revision). If sourcing locks at a node other than the witness (explicitly allowed: `model.yaml` "may differ from the promise's witness node"), the witness keeps a confirmed unit of capacity it will never ship, and the real node's book is not charged. `promise-model.md:147-149` claims "the network never promises more orders onto a vendor's 18:00 pickup than that vendor said it can ship", which is true only if sourcing never moves anything. Linehaul's departure booking does use the real node (BookOnLock, `model.yaml:1216`), so vehicle capacity is right and the order-capacity book is not. Not registered.
- **cosmetic: model text contradicts itself and the build.** `model.yaml:809` says "cross-region is allowed only when it is the only way", while G26 (register, Built) decided it is a penalty and "the business decision text is corrected". It was not corrected. Allowed by the register's own "Built = not yet edited" rule, but the page reads as a contradiction.
- **G12 residual still open as recorded.** An acceptance after cancel of a RELEASED assignment is only logged (`model.yaml` policy `AcceptanceAfterCancelOnlyLogged`, register row G12). Known; not re-reported as new.

### Inferences
- Sourcing's strongest idea is the virtual queue. Its weakness is that it sits between two capacity ledgers (book, departures) and only reconciles one of them.

### Gaps
- I did not measure how often candidate differs from witness; no runtime data exists.

## Availability and promise: ATP vs CTP vs available-to-sell, reservation vs allocation, input owners

### Takeaway
Promise inputs come from sensible owners and the hold-then-confirm mechanism is sound for *capacity*. For *stock*, three things diverge from the ATP concept: confirmed units are not netted, capacity is folded into the stock number without time-phasing, and the stock-to-promise page's headline guarantees ("overselling prevented", "zero, never a guess") are partly false as written.

### Cited Findings
- **OK: input owners.** Handling time, cutoffs and hours come from the vendor via `node-topology`; departures from `linehaul`; waves from `last-mile`; grade from `node-performance`; quantity from `offer-availability`. All are event-fed, with no synchronous call on the hot path (`promise-model.md:37-49`, `model.yaml` integration rules). Cross-checked: the AsyncAPI consumer lists and Kafka adapters agree with the model (see the joint section).
- **OK: the promise concept.** "Witness path plus capacity held at promise time" (`promise-model.md:107-149`, `adr/0008`) is a real CTP-like check with `basis` and `NOMINAL` honesty. The evolution note calls it "arguably more explicit than anything Amazon publishes", which I cannot verify but do not dispute conceptually.
- **error (N3): units of a CONFIRMED promise are not netted.** `Available = listing qty - units of PROVISIONAL promises` (`delivery-promise:docs/adr/0002-aggregates-and-invariants.md:56`; `delivery-promise:internal/adapters/outbound/postgres/repositories.go:201-202`; `network.go:161-174`). The hold on units therefore disappears at confirm, yet the WMS reserves nothing until the vendor sees the PO after `releaseAt`. In the docs' own worked example the order is placed at 14:10 and the fleet does not raise the held order until 15:56 (`order-assignment-model.md:143-146`), so for about 1h45, plus up to about 6 minutes of recompute and feed, those units are sellable again. `order-sourcing` nets nothing either (`order-sourcing:internal/domain/network/network.go:199`, `CanShip` compares raw `AvailableQty`). The failure is caught later: the vendor rejects (`OUT_OF_STOCK`) and sourcing reassigns or re-promises, so it surfaces as a broken or revised promise rather than a shipped oversell. `[memory]` In ATP terms, a committed order must reduce uncommitted supply until the warehouse's own allocation takes over. **`stock-to-promise.md:303-309` states "overselling is prevented by local bookkeeping, not by the feed catching up", and :506-507 "correctness does not depend on the feed at all". Both are false after confirm.** Not registered.
- **error (N2): capacity is folded into the stock number and not time-phased.** `advertised = min(usable, throughput of the next feasible cutoff)` per SKU (`network-fulfillment:internal/application/usecases/recompute_capability_offers.go:132-184`; ADR 0031 at :64 "what can ship on the next truck"). ADR 0031 walks forward only when *cycle time* rules a cutoff out (`:138`, `for !anyPathFits`). A cutoff whose capacity is *used up* gives `total=0, anyKnown=true` (`:184`), a proven zero with basis `THROUGHPUT_CONSTRAINED`, for **every SKU in the same pass**. Downstream, `ITEM_QUANTITY_NOT_AVAILABLE` then applies to the whole quote for every tier (`stock-to-promise.md:297-301`). So a full next truck would make the whole site unsellable, including for a `STANDARD` order that could leave on a later truck. This is the same "dark catalogue" symptom ADR 0031 fixed for the other cause (its lines 36-47 describe the live incident). The `PromiseBook` search already does this correctly per cutoff by moving to the next feasible one (`promise-model.md:68-70`). The offer also does not say which cutoff it was computed against (ADR 0031:151, acknowledged as a consequence). Also, the same throughput figure caps each SKU independently, so the sum over SKUs can exceed it. Static inference from code; not run.
- **debatable: conflation of ATS and CTP.** `availableQty` plus `basis` mixes a stock claim (ATP/ATS) with a capacity claim (CTP) in one integer, and capacity is then counted again in `PromiseBook` via `cutoffs[].maxOrders`. Having capacity in two places is what makes N2 possible. Keeping `basis` is honest labelling (`model.yaml`, offer-availability decisions). `[memory]` The literature separates ATP from CTP because they answer different questions.
- **debatable: units vs orders.** `maxOrders` and `throughputCap{unit: ORDER}` are filled from path capacity in *units* (`network-fulfillment:internal/application/usecases/profile_derivation.go:161`, `units, known := remaining(...)`; `:179` `throughputPerDay` "most orders"; translation at `ubiquitous-language.md`, "Throughput Cap / cutoff maxOrders | remaining path capacity"). G9 records the orders/packages conversion on the network side, not this units/orders one. Unit semantics of `PathCapacityChanged` are the fleet's; I read only the variable name and doc comment here.
- **error, doc only (N7): stock-to-promise failure semantics are wrong in three rows.**
  - `:513` "Usable lookup fails: SKU skipped, then omitted from the feed - **zeroed**, never sent stale", and `:194-195`. The code does the opposite on purpose: a stale SKU makes the feed *not complete*, so the network does **not** zero it and the old figure stays live (`network-fulfillment:internal/application/usecases/feed_plan.go:98`, `Complete` is false when `len(plan.Stale) != 0`, plus the function's doc comment "must not be left out of one").
  - `:525-526` "The pattern across every row: degradation goes to zero... never to a guess that keeps selling". This contradicts its own rows :514 (`known=false` falls back to physical) and :517 (feed disabled, keeps selling) and the point above.
  - `:505-506` "`asOf` travels with every figure so any consumer can refuse one past its shelf life". `delivery-promise` uses `AsOf` only for ordering (`projections.go:188`); no shelf-life check exists, and an hour-old figure is by design (`:190-192`).
- **OK: the rest of that page.** The formula, hop list, hold-back ("repeats an unchanged one at most hourly", `feed_inventory.go:22-25`) and the p95 gate rules match the code I read.
- **Reservation vs allocation terms (OK).** Fleet hold is a real allocation: `network-fulfillment:internal/application/usecases/receive_network_demand.go` step 6 "so inventory reservations never outlive the decision". Network holds are soft. This two-level structure is right in principle; N3 is the gap between the levels.
- **scope gap, unstated.** No future supply in the figure: ATP in the literature includes scheduled receipts ([Oracle ATP](https://docs.oracle.com/cd/A60725_05/html/comnls/us/mrp/atp01.htm); `[memory]` APICS). Here it is on-hand only, with no pre-order or backorder. Reasonable for a study, but undeclared.
- **Registered and verified still open.** G47: `delivery-promise` sums across owners (`network.go:169`) while `order-sourcing` takes the first listing it finds (`sourcing.go:102`), and both contradict `model.yaml:377` ("quantities of different owners are never summed"), so the disagreement is now concrete in code. G48: `offer-availability` has no `SellerSuspended` reference. G51: see Inbound section. G61 looks **stale in the register**: `orderByAt` was merged to `delivery-promise` on 2026-11-05 (`apis/openapi.yaml:642`; commit `8391f4a`), but the register still says Open. G60: no `delivery-estimates` route found, still open.

### Inferences
- The fix conceptually splits into: net confirmed-but-unallocated units until the vendor's feed reflects them; keep `availableQty` stock-only or make it per-cutoff; let the book carry capacity.

### Gaps
- APICS wording could not be retrieved. The units semantics of `PathCapacityChanged` belong to the warehouse-internal researcher.

## Transportation: linehaul, middle mile, last mile, sort, routing, shipping vs TMS, tracking events, POD

### Takeaway
The transport split (schedule and capacity in linehaul, waves and routes in last mile, label and timeline in shipping-and-tracking) is valid and clean. Three semantics are weak: LOST is reachable from only one state, no return movement exists, and proof of delivery is not modelled beyond an outcome and timestamp. Load building is manual with no automation trigger.

### Cited Findings
- **OK: linehaul.** Scheduled departures with capacity, cutoff before departure before arrival, loads consolidated at the origin, a horizon roll (`model.yaml` linehaul policies, `:1215-1216`). This is a defensible owned-network TMS subset. No tender, rate or freight audit (the TMS functions at [Umbrex](https://umbrex.com/resources/transportation-management-system-playbook/core-tms-capabilities-and-modules/)), consistent with parcel nodes being network-operated (`adr/0004`). Not declared as an exclusion, though `business-capability-map.md:66` says "keep optimization behind a port".
- **OK: last mile.** Waves with induct cutoff, window, stop capacity by distinct stop; routes per partner van; three attempts then return (`model.yaml:1352`; `order-assignment-model.md`). Capacity by stop, not package, is the right unit for delivery routing (`[memory]` VRP framing). Routing itself is manual `PlanRoute`, stated as "routing replaceable" in `business-capability-map.md:70`.
- **OK: shipping-and-tracking is rightly distinct from TMS.** It holds the label and the customer timeline consolidated from others' facts, not transport planning; [Logistics Viewpoints](https://logisticsviewpoints.com/transportation-management-systems-tms-buyers-guide/) lists visibility alongside TMS, so either placement is acceptable. Carrier is a fixed rule by tier (`shipping-and-tracking:internal/domain/label/label.go:56-66`, `RN-EXPRESS`, `RN-GROUND`), with no carrier selection and no carrier integration. This is honest for a closed network, but "carrier" here is a label, not an external party. Debatable naming, no contract issue.
- **Tracking event plausibility (OK with exceptions).** The sequence LABELLED, HANDED_OVER, IN_TRANSIT, AT_STATION, OUT_FOR_DELIVERY, DELIVERED plus NO_SCAN exception, and out-of-order scan handling, is realistic (`model.yaml` shipping-and-tracking; `ExceptionOnSilence`).
- **error (N8): LOST reachable only from IN_TRANSIT.** `model.yaml:1462`; `shipping-and-tracking:internal/domain/journey/journey.go:252-253`. A package lost after handover but before a departure scan (HANDED_OVER), at the station (AT_STATION), or on the van (OUT_FOR_DELIVERY) cannot be declared lost; the `closeFrom` guard refuses it. Loss on the last mile is common in practice (`[memory]`; no citation retrieved), and `delivery-promise`'s `LOST` judgement depends on this event, so a promise for such a package stays open. Not registered.
- **scope gap (N9): proof of delivery is absent.** `DeliveryAttempted` carries `routeId, trackingNumber, outcome, failureReason, at` (`last-mile-delivery:apis/asyncapi.yaml:408-412`; `internal/domain/route/route.go:74-75`). Grep for proof, signature, photo returned nothing in either service. No POD artefact, recipient or geo. Study scope acceptable, but undeclared, and the user question names POD explicitly.
- **scope gap (already known in the register).** G11 and `model.yaml:1479`: "How a delivery partner learns the address at the door is not decided". Still open as recorded. It is a large hole for last-mile semantics but is registered, so not new.

## Inbound: seller-inbound, vendor-connectivity, ASN/PO/EDI, handoff to inbound-receiving

### Takeaway
The ASN semantics are correct and the handoff is well built on paper: manifest pre-announced, SSCC/GTIN correct, carton-level receipt returned. But in live mode the poll is broken by a contract change (N1), and received seller stock never becomes sellable (G51, verified still open). The vendor PO is the correct direct-fulfillment analogue only for vendor-owned stock.

### Cited Findings
- **OK: EDI mapping (my own mapping; the repo does not state it).** `GET /vendor/purchase-orders` = 850; `POST /vendor/acknowledgements` fill-or-kill = 855; `POST /vendor/inventory` = 846; `POST /vendor/shipment-confirmations` = 856 outbound; `GET /vendor/inbound-shipments` = inbound 856/DESADV; `POST /vendor/inbound-receipts` = 861/944. The protocol matches [Direct Fulfillment's order](https://www.stacksync.com/edi/amazon/amazon-dropship-direct-fulfillment/x12-850-purchase-order). G44 (JSON only) remains open as registered.
- **OK: SSCC and GTIN rules.** 18 digits with mod-10, uniqueness, carton as logistic unit, GTIN length handling, mixed carton without pack GTIN (`model.yaml` seller-inbound invariants) agree with [GS1 UK](https://www.gs1uk.org/support-training/help-to-use-and-implement-gs1-standards/logistics-labels-and-ssccs). Recording rather than correcting discrepancies is the right posture.
- **debatable: PO semantics for seller-owned stock.** For vendor-owned stock, a PO per shipment group is correct drop-ship semantics. For stock a seller owns inside a vendor node, the vendor is acting as a 3PL, and the corresponding instruction is a warehouse shipping order (940/945), not a purchase ([Racklify](https://racklify.com/encyclopedia/edi-856-vs-edi-940945-when-to-use-each/)). Moot until G47 and G51 close, but the term will mislead when they do.
- **OK: handoff to inbound-receiving, on paper.** `network-fulfillment:internal/application/usecases/intake_inbound_shipments.go:191-199` builds an ASN, keeps cartons and SSCCs, resolves GTIN through product-master and carries `ownerRef`. `return_inbound_receipts.go` builds the receipt from local projections. - **G51 verified still open (S1).** `network-fulfillment:internal/adapters/outbound/inventoryclient/client.go:62` calls `/inventory/{sku}/usable` with no `ownerRef`, and `inventory-storage:internal/adapters/inbound/http/stock_handler.go:89-95` scopes to the owner given. ## Inventory across the network: who owns position; single-writer; inter-warehouse transfer

### Takeaway
The single-writer rule holds. `inventory-storage` is the only writer of physical stock; `offer-availability` holds a vendor *claim*, written only through the feed; `network-inventory-planning` never edits stock. These are different quantities with different owners, not two sources of truth. No context owns a retail-wide inventory position in the planning sense, and the word "network" means two different things.

### Cited Findings
- **OK: one writer.** `warehouse-docs/docs/contexts/network-inventory-planning/index.md`: "`inventory-storage` stays the authority for physical stock and reservations"; NIP's saga "never moves stock itself". `offer-availability` model: "the quantity each node currently *claims*", basis kept and never re-derived. `model.yaml:35` hard boundary means no shared read. Direction is one way, fleet to network through the feed. Verified: a grep for `warehouse.<x>.events|analytics` and `com.warehouse.` over non-test Go in all eleven network repos returns zero files, and no `retailnetwork.*` type appears in the NIP or warehouse-planning AsyncAPI type lists.
- **OK: the transfer saga matches the textbook shape.** Operator approval, reservation at origin (`TransferStockAllocated`, `...Rejected`), pick and dispatch work demands, physical facts (`TransferPicked`, `TransferDispatched`, `TransferArrived`), receipt and stow close it (`network-inventory-planning:apis/asyncapi.yaml`). Usable at origin drops on reservation and rises at destination after stow, so the feed reflects transfers with no extra coupling. In-transit units are unsellable, conservative and correct.
- **debatable: `order-sourcing` cannot see in-transit stock, and the network cannot tell a transfer is coming.** Follows from the hard boundary; correct for a separate organisation.
- **scope gap: NIP is mostly advisory plumbing today.** Its own page says not built: dynamic order-site assignment, demand forecasting, safety stock, a persisted lane and policy catalogue, and "the scheduled rebalance therefore records zero proposals today". Honestly stated. `[memory]` Multi-echelon positioning and risk pooling (Simchi-Levi) have no realisation anywhere. `domain-vision.md` declares placement is the vendor's decision, and G49 registers seller placement.
- **error, limits the joint: single site.** `RecomputeCapabilityOffers.SiteId` is one site (`recompute_capability_offers.go:47-49`, "inherited simplification"). `cmd/netfulfil/capability.go:62` wires `siteId()` while the inventory feed, profile and inbound loops take `Sites` (`inventory_feed.go:69`, `profile.go:68`, `inbound.go:150`). `GET /inventory/{sku}/usable` takes no site parameter (`stock_handler.go:81-95`). So the fleet can only be one node for stock purposes (`SITE_ID=site-1`, `warehouse-infra:helm-values/network-fulfillment.yaml:50-51`), yet NIP exists to move stock between sites and `node-model.md` maps fifteen roles. Disclosed in the fleet canvas (`docs/contexts/network-fulfillment/bounded-context-canvas.md:253`) but not on the network's integration pages, whose `N-VWS-AUS3` and `DFW7` examples suggest many sites.
- **cosmetic: term collision.** Fleet "network" (its own sites and lanes, `network-inventory-planning`) versus "retail network" (another organisation), and "lane" in both. `[memory]` A reader coming from the literature will assume NIP is the retail network's inventory planner.

### Inferences
- There are three stock quantities, correctly layered: physical plus reservations (`inventory-storage`), claim (`offer-availability`), and soft hold (`delivery-promise`). N3 is the only leak in the layering.

### Gaps
- Whether a multi-site `inventory-storage` read exists elsewhere was not traced; this is the warehouse-internal researcher's area.

## Capacity and performance: node-performance vs warehouse-planning vs process-path-management, feedback loops

### Takeaway
The network's own loop (measure, grade, handicap and cost) is well designed. Three things weaken it: on-time lateness is charged to the node without attribution, the vendor never reads its own scorecard, and on the fleet side the planning horizon is not connected to the declared capacity.

### Cited Findings
- **OK: measured vs declared.** `node-performance` computes only from observed facts; handover is measured by the network's first scan, not vendor `shippedAt` (`node-performance:docs/adr/0002-aggregates-and-invariants.md:107-110`, A6). A handicap slows but never drops a node, and suspension stays human (`promise-model.md:101-103`). Sound.
- **debatable (N11): `LATE` broken promises all count against the node.** `node-performance:docs/adr/0002-aggregates-and-invariants.md:123-132`, A8: "`LATE` (delivered after `deliverByLatest`, a node's handover is the usual reason) counts against the node". A late linehaul or a re-attempted delivery lowers a vendor's grade, and that grade then handicaps future promises (`model.yaml:1566`). The handover metric is already properly attributed, so `promiseKept` double-counts node lateness and adds network lateness. G33 fixes the vocabulary, not the attribution; that ADR itself flags the mismatch as a model gap. Not registered as an attribution problem.
- **debatable: open loop on the vendor side.** `GET /vendor/nodes/{nodeId}/scorecard` exists, but a grep of `network-fulfillment` Go code for scorecard returns nothing. `warehouse-planning` and `process-path-management` get no signal from it. Neither `cycleTimeP95` (an operator-configured catalogue value: `stock-to-promise.md:367-368`) nor capacity is corrected by network-measured outcomes.
- **debatable: three capacity notions, unconnected at the top.** `warehouse-planning` (plan horizon: `CapacityPlanPublished`, consumed by NIP), `wes-work-planning` (operational `PathCapacityChanged`, consumed by NF), and network `PromiseBook` (declared). `CapacityShortageDetected` has no route to the declared profile. `warehouse-planning/context-map.md:84` marks `network-fulfillment` (demand) as "intended", and `business-context.md:82` as "a documented later decision". Network demand reaches planning only via `OrderAllocated` after PO acceptance, so queued demand and any forecast are invisible. `[memory]` S&OP or CPFR style sharing is the usual remedy. A hierarchy of planning vs operational capacity is itself standard, and the gap is honest in fleet docs but not mentioned on the network's pages.
- **OK: ratchet noted.** `DeclareNodeProfiles` keeps the larger of observed and declared ceiling (ADR 0031 consequences), which avoids dips, though it also means a declared capacity never falls by itself: a debatable side effect, in tension with "promises never exceed what a vendor declared it can ship" only in the sense that the declaration is stale-high.
- **G52** (inbound metrics mix causes) still open as registered; those metrics are informational only.

### Inferences
- `promiseKept` should be attributed before it grades. The handover metric shows the team already knows how.

### Gaps
- The warehouse-internal correctness of p95 and path capacity is another researcher's scope.
