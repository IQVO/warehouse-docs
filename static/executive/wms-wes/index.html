part 1 · the business · why it exists

A warehouse core that keeps stock true and decides when work runs

This is the warehouse itself: everything that gets goods through the building. The WMS side books the door, tracks the trailer, receives and stows stock, keeps every bin true, refills pick faces, handles returns and loads the outbound trailer. The WES side releases allocated orders in cutoff order, lets stations pull tasks, scores labor and opens an owned case when something goes wrong.

Today

Stock, dock and yard, order release and floor tasks each live in their own context, joined by events on one broker.

The catch

The fleet's 'wes' label is wider than the industry's WES. There is no WCS, no lot or serial traceability, and every endpoint is open.

part 1 · the business · against the market

Against the big WMS vendors the design direction matches, but the reach is partial

PartialWMS and WES softwarevs Manhattan, Blue Yonder, SAP EWM, Körber, SSI Schäfer

Where we match or lead

  • Waveless, cutoff-ordered release and pull dispatch, the direction Manhattan and Blue Yonder describe
  • Chaotic-storage stow with scan checks, revocable reservations and hazmat placement rules
  • Yard facts kept apart from the door calendar, so ship-confirm follows the trailer's departure
  • People approve transfers, slot plans and labor moves; the fleet only proposes them

Gaps to close

  • High No WCS or robotics orchestration; only a reserved seam in fulfillment-execution (ADR 0015)
  • High No lot, serial or expiry (FEFO) traceability in the stock model
  • High No demand forecasting or multi-echelon optimisation; planning runs on deterministic rules
  • Medium Replenishment and interleaving chains not traced end to end; REPLENISH path is seeded by hand

Leader capabilities are public research and vendor-published material; gaps reflect repo docs reviewed in November 2026. Our judgement, not a certification.

part 2 · architecture · the ground rules

Five architecture principles keep every context small and independent

Each context owns its data

One database per context, plus an analytical one; no shared tables, no sibling imports. Cross-context facts are events or calls.

One envelope, one outbox

Every Kafka message is a CloudEvent, committed with the change through a transactional outbox before the relay sends it.

The domain depends on nothing

Hexagonal ports and adapters, and an architecture fitness test fails the build if an adapter reaches into the domain.

Agents advise, people act

MCP tools and A2A skills declare an authority level; the ops agent is read-only apart from two human-confirmed act slices.

Build where it differentiates

Core effort goes to stock, release, dispatch and planning; configuration, layout and labor standards stay Generic or Supporting.

this service owns the triage, assignment, SLA and resolution of that case, never the producer's own state. fulfillment-execution: Core — Pick, pack and SLAM task lifecycle with pull-based claimNext part 2 · architecture · the decisions that shaped it

Six decisions shaped it: no waves, pull dispatch, stock truth, yard facts, a network wall

wes-work-planning ADR 0002

Waveless, continuous release

Release one unit at a time by cutoff instead of batching waves; the model the industry's WES layer emerged to serve.

fulfillment-execution ADR 0002, 0003

Pull dispatch with leased claims

Stations ask for the best-fit task; an unconfirmed claim expires back to the pool, so work cannot be stranded.

fulfillment-execution ADR 0015

Equipment control as a reserved seam

No WCS is built; the seam is reserved so equipment can be added without touching dispatch rules.

inventory-storage ADR 0002, 0003

Chaotic storage and revocable reservations

Items go to any free legal bin, tracked to the bin; reservations can be revoked so held stock is never lost.

yard docs YD-0004

Physical facts win

Only the yard records arrival, docking and departure; the calendar and ship-confirm follow, so shipped means shipped.

network-fulfillment ADR 0001

One context owns the network wall

Network demand, its acknowledgement clock and its vocabulary stay in one anti-corruption layer, not inside order-management.

part 2 · architecture · what could go wrong · and what we do about it

The biggest risks are open endpoints, no equipment layer and chains never traced end to end

Every endpoint is open

Static bearer keys were adopted, then removed fleet-wide by decision; every REST and MCP call is unauthenticated.

→

Reachable only inside the cluster today; bringing auth back needs a new owner decision and an ADR.

No equipment layer

With no WCS, release reacts to backlog and capacity, not to equipment state, and no robot or conveyor can be driven.

→

An anti-corruption seam is reserved in fulfillment-execution (ADR 0015); no adapter is built.

Chains not traced end to end

Replenishment and interleaving are built hop by hop but not traced on the cluster; the REPLENISH path is an operator seed.

→

Tracked as open rows in the fleet gap tracker; the next step is one real request through every hop.

Built but dark, and thinly watched

Yard MCP, reports and sweeps are switched off on the cluster; several newer contexts emit no metrics or traces.

→

The infra switches are already built; each context's docs name a metrics registry as the next step.

part 2 · architecture · what comes next

Next we light up what is built, prove the open chains, then reach for equipment

warehouse-systems · ecosystem briefing · November 2026wms-wes
Light up what is built: Switch on the yard and dock MCP servers, report services and sweeps that are built but off on the cluster, and apply their metrics. Trace the open chains: Run one replenishment request and one interleaving hint through every hop on the cluster; the REPLENISH seed is still an operator step. Close the shipped-fact loop: ShipmentConfirmed to order-management and network-fulfillment is wired but unused; it should replace the manual shipment confirmation. Qualify and instrument the newest: Run the industry tests on the contexts without a verdict, and add metrics and traces to the newest contexts. Reach for equipment and traceability: Later: a WCS adapter behind the reserved seam, and lot, serial and expiry attributes with a FEFO draw on reservations (new ADR).
